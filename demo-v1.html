The whole lifecycle in ten steps. A publisher and two invokers onboard securely and get identities with trust chains; the publisher registers capabilities, policies and token rates; an invoker discovers the catalogue, signs a smart contract, gets user consent and calls through the enforcement point, with pass and fail decisions, receipts on both sides, a shared ledger and settlement in tokens. Each company is an isolated tenant. Other views go deeper into each part.

Where this is heading: a trusted marketplace for agents. Browse certified listings, state an intent instead of naming a tool, see how the platform routes it within every rule, and how limits, certification, bounties, wallets and disputes work. Signatures and proofs are real; providers, tokens and payouts are simulated.

Metrics and KPIs. Aggregated across tenants for the platform operator, or scoped to one tenant to show isolation. Filter by publisher, invoker, category, outcome and time, and group by any dimension. Data is illustrative, plus the calls you make in this demo.

You publish an MCP server. Walk through the six steps to put Saktum in front of it: connect, let agents sign in properly, hand out keys, enforce limits, see who calls what, and verify a signed receipt for every call.

Agent ID: who stands behind an agent. Register an agent, link it to the organisation that owns it and the person who manages it, prove its identity on every call, build reputation from its receipts, and anchor its history so nobody can rewrite it. Credentials and proofs here are really signed and checked in your browser.

Trust ledger and payments. Smart-contract ideas without putting decisions on a chain: signed agreements, programmable mandates, pay-per-call with tokens, escrow on proven outcomes, selective disclosure of credentials, and a consortium ledger that no single party can rewrite. Signatures and hashes are real; tokens and settlement are simulated, with no real money.

Consent, rules and money. How people stay in control of their agents, how rules are written and changed safely, how one call is checked on both sides, and how publishers and the platform earn. Consent receipts, policy bundles and cross-signed receipts are really signed; operator events and revenue figures are illustrative.

Coming next: controls for teams running agents. Pick something for the support-ops agent to do. Every tool call goes through the mediator, which decides, explains its decision, and writes a receipt signed in your browser. Change one character in a receipt and verification fails.
